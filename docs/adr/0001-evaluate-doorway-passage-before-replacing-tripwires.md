# Evaluate doorway passage counting before replacing tripwires

Store traffic means actual passage between outside and inside, not movement toward an entrance. Explore an isolated, single-doorway prototype using manually annotated outside, doorway, and inside zones, with directional motion as supporting evidence rather than a minimum-speed requirement. Keep production tripwire counting unchanged until comparison against independently labeled store-passage events demonstrates improvement; MOT pedestrian annotations alone do not establish entry/exit accuracy.

The alternative is to replace tripwires immediately with a doorway-region and velocity heuristic, but that risks relabeling approaches and disappearances as entries without establishing passage. Evaluate individual events by doorway and direction using precision and recall, with unresolved predictions on confirmed passages counted as misses and human-uncertain passages reported separately.

For the first prototype, require outside, doorway, and inside observations in order under one continuous track identity, without a missing-observation gap during the candidate passage. Do not stitch different track IDs or interpolate an unobserved doorway visit. Count resulting misses against human-confirmed events and distinguish tracking failures from counting failures during analysis; these conservative rules may need revisiting if they prevent adequate recall.

Evaluate both manually checked foot-point trajectories and the unchanged detector/tracker pipeline. Only end-to-end results can justify production replacement. The initial replacement gate permits no regression in entry precision, entry recall, exit precision, or exit recall, and requires a meaningful improvement in at least one; neither aggregate scores nor net totals can conceal a regression.

Separate development footage from held-out scoring footage and freeze geometry, noise handling, motion estimation, and event matching before scoring. Use video time and one-to-one matching against human-labeled traversal intervals for the same doorway and direction; duplicates are errors. Any additional timing tolerance must be fixed using development footage. Report insufficient evidence if the clip lacks enough confirmed events in either direction.

The existing birdseye clip is exploratory material, not the acceptance benchmark. Its metadata reports 29.01 seconds at approximately 29.97 FPS and 3840x2160; one-second samples around Studio F do not establish a reliably observable inside zone or sufficient confirmed bidirectional passage. This sampling is not a complete event annotation and does not establish that the clip contains no passages. Require additional doorway-visible footage before tuning the passage counter or claiming improved accuracy; use the current clip for exploratory geometry and passerby checks only.

Whether to drop tripwires remains open. Benchmark size, numerical improvement margins, timing tolerance, boundary-noise handling, motion-estimation settings, and production migration policy remain gated decisions, not approved implementation details. Continue the design interview after suitable footage is available; no production replacement or counter implementation is authorized by this ADR.
